About the series

How do financial investors and the shift to financialisation as a governing mechanism change contemporary health systems? In three videos, professor David Blumenthal (Harvard University), professor Erin Fuse Brown (Brown University) and Rob Coppedge (CEO ECHO Health Ventures), discuss how financialisation affects healthcare, how it engages with the evolvement of artificial intelligence, and how regulation could and should protect healthcare.

The Role of Investors in Health Systems

In this segment, the panel traces how the United States healthcare system arrived at its current state: a market so large that, measured by GDP, it would rank among the world's major economies. The panel examines what financialisation means in practice. What actually changes when investors acquire a hospital or a nursing home? Where do the cuts land first, and what does the evidence show about the consequences for staff and patients?

Technology and AI: Accelerator and New Frontier

Artificial intelligence has entered a healthcare system already restructured by private capital, and in this segment the panel asks whether it is being deployed as a tool for better care or primarily as a new mechanism for extracting revenue. What happens as healthcare entities and technology companies converge, and the boundary between protected health data and commercial consumer data dissolves?

Regulation: Why the US-Europe Gap Matters

In this segment, the panel discusses the issue of regulation. In Europe, capital flows into dentistry, ophthalmology and long-term care — the sectors without price controls. In the United States, where hospitals and physician services are largely unregulated, the entire system is open. The panel examines why that regulation is so hard to create and enforce whilst also a necessary market condition as it will create the trust that markets cannot generate alone.
